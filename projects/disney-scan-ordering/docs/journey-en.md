# Shanghai Disney App | Ordering Experience Map

A task journey reconstructed from design materials. Questions, emotions and pain points are hypotheses for review, not findings from completed user research or measured customer behavior.

## 1. Scenario

A guest needs food during a park visit, enters through the app or an on-site QR code, chooses a restaurant and meals for themselves or companions, and needs clear guidance about the next action. Family ordering is an analytical scenario, not a verified dominant segment.

## 2. Experience map

| Dimension | Discover | Scan / select restaurant | Enter menu | Browse / configure | Review / payment entry | Order / fulfillment |
| Goal | Find mobile ordering | Reach the correct restaurant or table service | Start ordering | Select meals for companions | Confirm choices and amount | Find order and next steps |
| Touchpoints | Dashboard, map scanner, physical code | Code type, list, restaurant detail | Login, operating status, HTML menu | Categories, item cards, meal sheet, options | Cart, amount, Payment button | My Food Order, confirmation, redemption entry |
| Question — hypothesis | Where can I order? | Is this the right restaurant? | Is it available now? | What is still required? | Are my choices and total correct? | Where is my order? |
| Emotion — hypothesis | Hungry / time constrained | Hopeful, or uncertain about the code | Reassured by access, frustrated by interruption | Engaged, but potentially overloaded | Cautious before committing | Reassured by clear status, anxious without guidance |
| Risk | Unclear entry points | Wrong code or service context | Lost login context, dead-end closure page | Adult / child confusion, missed specifications | Lost configuration, inconsistent total, repeated submission | Missing status or unclear next action |
| Opportunity | Align entry labels in both languages | Route by code type; identify restaurant | Preserve intent; offer alternatives | Show group progress and preserve choices | Review restaurant, options, quantity and amount | Maintain accessible order entry and explicit next action |
| Evidence boundary | Entry concepts supplied | Routing flow supplied | Login and operating-state branches supplied | Menu and configuration screens supplied | Payment entry visible; full flow unresolved | Entry descriptions supplied; full fulfillment unresolved |

## 3. Critical moments

Correct service entry: successful scanning must lead to the intended restaurant and service context. General and location-specific codes have different purposes.

Complete meal configuration: groups, quantities and drink options require visible progress, clear validation and recoverable choices.

Continuity after payment entry: additional original material is required to establish the complete payment and fulfillment experience.

## 4. Review priorities

First: correct routing, closed-location recovery, login continuation, required-option validation and access to order results.

Next: configuration persistence, consistent amounts, complete translations and efficient menu browsing. Priorities are proposals to assess against actual volume, issue data and implementation cost.

## 5. Validation and leadership

Suggested task checks include selecting a restaurant from a general code, opening a location code, recovering from a closed restaurant, configuring adult and child drinks, revising options and locating an order. Record completion, errors, requests for assistance and participants’ own descriptions; use research evidence to validate emotional hypotheses.

The leadership story is the work of organizing multiple entries and service contexts, translating meal complexity into interaction rules, and aligning bilingual embedded experiences across teams. No unsupported conversion or efficiency claims are attached.
